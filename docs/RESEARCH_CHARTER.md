I want to seriously attack the Voynich Manuscript with modern AI, with the actual long-term goal of deciphering it rather than just doing another statistical analysis project.

The basic mindset is: do not assume Voynich is impossible just because people have failed for a century. Recent AI results in math, science, coding, and cryptanalysis show that old unsolved problems can become tractable when a strong model can search hypotheses, write code, run experiments, and iterate. Be extremely skeptical of results and demand verification, but do not prematurely dismiss ambitious hypotheses.

## Core idea

Voynichese is highly structured and clearly not simple random gibberish, but we do not actually know what the basic units mean or whether every observed unit carries information.

A major hypothesis I want to investigate is:

**Voynich may contain real encoded information mixed with structured pseudolanguage / null material.**

The filler does not need to look random. It could obey the exact same statistical and morphological rules as normal Voynichese, making it difficult for an outsider to distinguish.

It could be:

- deterministic filler,
- pseudorandom filler,
- periodic or shifting filler,
- context-dependent filler,
- words generated by modifying nearby real words,
- filler at the glyph level instead of the word level,
- or some mixture.

An intended reader could still read the manuscript if they knew the language, encoding conventions, or could recognize meaningless material from context.

This could help explain why Voynich has strong language-like statistics while still resisting normal decipherment techniques.

## Important related hypothesis: mappings may not be one-to-one

Do not assume:

Voynich word X = plaintext word Y.

Possible structures include:

- many Voynich forms representing the same latent unit;
- one plaintext letter/syllable/word mapping to many different Voynich forms;
- verbose homophonic encoding;
- abbreviations;
- semantic classes instead of literal words;
- one Voynich form having different meanings depending on context or manuscript section;
- state-dependent mappings.

For example, the same Voynich token could theoretically mean one thing on a pharmaceutical page and something completely different on a botanical page if the intended reader could resolve the meaning from context.

This matters because normal frequency analysis assumes a relatively rigid mapping. Modern neural models can instead represent probability distributions and contextual meanings.

## Idea 1: train models to recover signal from structured gibberish

Build synthetic mystery languages from known languages and artificial grammars.

Randomize the alphabet and transformations so a model cannot memorize English, Latin, etc.

Create examples like:

plaintext  
→ unknown alphabet/cipher  
→ structured filler insertion  
→ observed mystery text

Train a model to recover which material is information-bearing and reconstruct the latent sequence.

Make the filler increasingly difficult:

- random nonsense;
- valid-looking pseudowords;
- morphologically correct fake words;
- real words used in semantically wrong places;
- copy/mutate filler;
- deterministic filler;
- shifting/stateful filler.

The important benchmark would be whether the model works on a completely unseen language, unseen alphabet, and unseen filler mechanism.

If it can do that, apply it to Voynich.

The goal is not simply “detect weird words.” It is to learn something closer to:

**Which parts of an unknown symbolic sequence contribute to a coherent latent message?**

## Idea 2: train a tiny model entirely on Voynich

Train a deliberately small modern language model from scratch whose entire textual universe is the Voynich Manuscript.

No pretrained English knowledge. No Latin. No outside text.

Its job is simply to become extremely good at predicting/generating Voynichese.

Older projects have already trained small GPT/LSTM-style models on Voynich and generated plausible-looking text, so generation alone is not the interesting part.

The interesting part is using **modern mechanistic interpretability** to reverse-engineer what the model learned.

Potential questions:

- Does it internally group near-identical Voynich forms together?
- Does it learn line-position states?
- Does it independently encode Currier A/B?
- Does it learn copy/mutate behavior?
- Does it learn hidden token classes?
- Does it learn something analogous to morphology?
- Does it appear to separate signal-like and filler-like material?

Use causal interpretability rather than just plotting attention maps: ablations, activation patching, feature steering, sparse representations, circuit tracing, etc.

Train many random seeds and look for structures that repeatedly emerge.

The core idea is:

**If a tiny neural network becomes “fluent” in Voynichese, inspect the computation it had to learn in order to become fluent.**

## Idea 3: combine synthetic ground truth with Voynich mech interp

This may be especially powerful.

Generate synthetic corpora where we know the true hidden process:

- natural language,
- homophonic cipher,
- verbose cipher,
- language + structured filler,
- copy/mutate pseudolanguage,
- stateful encoding,
- artificial language,
- etc.

Train the same tiny architecture on each.

Mechanistically interpret them.

Learn what internal circuits/features tend to arise when a model is learning:

- filler,
- homophony,
- state tracking,
- copying,
- morphological mutation,
- semantic ambiguity,
- and so on.

Then train the identical architecture on Voynich and compare.

If models trained on known “language + filler” worlds consistently develop one type of internal computation, and Voynich models develop something functionally similar, that could be meaningful evidence.

## Idea 4: use a powerful frontier model as the researcher, not necessarily as the Voynich LM

Astra-class reasoning/coding models should sit outside the experiment and act as the scientist.

The loop should look like:

observe pattern  
→ generate hypothesis  
→ write experiment/solver  
→ run it  
→ inspect results  
→ falsify or refine hypothesis  
→ repeat

This is similar to recent AI-assisted cryptanalysis results where the important capability was not merely language modeling, but persistent autonomous hypothesis search + coding + computation.

The tiny Voynich models remain small enough to inspect.

The large model does the research.

## Other important ideas

Do not assume Voynich spaces are real word boundaries.

Try glyph-level, learned-unit-level, token-level, line-level, and possibly paragraph-level representations.

Use manuscript context eventually:

- herbal pages,
- astronomical/zodiac material,
- pharmaceutical pages,
- bathing diagrams,
- labels,
- repeated visual structures.

Images can provide weak semantic constraints, but never force a translation from a guessed plant identification.

Repeated Voynich words and near-duplicate word families should be treated as major clues. It may be useful to determine whether the roughly ten thousand apparent surface forms collapse into a much smaller number of latent families.

One especially interesting test of the filler hypothesis is whether removing inferred filler reveals repeated phrases or stronger long-range dependencies that are currently hidden.

## Main caution

A model that predicts Voynich well does not necessarily recover the historical process that generated Voynich.

It may learn statistical shortcuts.

So synthetic ground-truth experiments, held-out pages, causal interventions, and independent verification are essential.

Similarly, do not let a decoder simply label every failure as “filler.”

Any proposed filler mechanism needs to generalize out of sample and outperform random deletion/control procedures.

## Overall objective

The broad strategy is:

1. understand the statistical/generative structure of Voynichese;
2. test whether some material is structured filler or null content;
3. identify latent equivalence classes and hidden states;
4. determine what kind of encoding/generative process could produce it;
5. use modern neural models, mechanistic interpretability, synthetic training worlds, and agentic experimentation to narrow the hypothesis space;
6. eventually attack the underlying plaintext/semantics.

The goal is not just to make Voynich-like text.

The goal is to **reverse-engineer the hidden process that produced Voynichese and use that to push toward an actual decipherment.**

Treat all of these as hypotheses, not truths. Challenge them aggressively. If a better explanation appears, follow it.

But do not give up on the actual decipherment simply because the problem is old or historically difficult.
