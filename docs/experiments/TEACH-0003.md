# TEACH-0003: dense scale and auxiliary row-alignment supervision

**Preregistration, 2026-09-23 UTC. No TEACH-0003 MPS benchmark, training, or evaluation has run.** [TEACH-0002](TEACH-0002-results.md) failed relational lookup: two-seed curricula chose visible candidates yet changed answers on only 0/128 and 0/128 first-hop query reversals, and 0/128 and 1/128 direct reversals; no correct four-way factorial quartet. That experiment used a 668,032-parameter transformer. The failure could reflect optimization, scale, the input/task format, or an architecture bias. It does **not** show that small transformers fundamentally cannot perform lookup. This successor tests (A) a dense capacity sweep under identical examples and schedule, and separately (B) whether supervised *learned* row alignment helps a large model with matched architecture, data, steps and near-matched compute. It is synthetic method qualification, not Voynich decipherment.

## Source basis and transfer limits

[Kaplan et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.08361) and [Hoffmann et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.15556) find substantial model/data/compute tradeoffs in language-model pretraining; their fitted laws do **not** predict performance or runtime for a 14-token episodic lookup task. The sweep tests scale empirically rather than extrapolating those laws. [Vaswani et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.03762) motivate the dense attention control. [Miller et al.](https://aclanthology.org/D16-1147/) separate key addressing from value reading in key-value memory networks, and [Santoro et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.01427) show relational modules can help on other structured tasks. Those works motivate a learned pointer-*supervision* condition here; this model does **not** implement their memory or relational-network architectures, and no fixed equality test, symbolic resolver or oracle lookup is inserted into inference. [Olsson et al.](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2022/in-context-learning-and-induction-heads/index.html) demonstrate copying-type circuits in transformers, but do not show that this experiment's model uses one. A later causal study is required before any circuit claim.

## Fresh generator and evaluation

Keep TEACH-0002's 14-token two-table grammar and 12 names/keys/objects so task difficulty is comparable, but use an independently frozen `TEACH-0003-v1/` SHA-256 family partition and new seeds. F families are unordered `(name set, key set)`; G families are `(key set, object set)`. Hash each into train buckets 0–7 or holdout buckets 8–9. Train only F-train/G-train. Randomize row assignments, table ordering, queried row and streamed episodes; repeated examples and recurring atomic relations are possible. A separate parser verifies every test answer.

The shared evaluation suite uses seed `63311`: 256 items each in four composed F/G train/holdout cells, first-hop train and holdout, direct train and holdout, held-out copy, 128 held-out F query-reversal pairs, 128 held-out G query-reversal pairs, and 128 four-way factorial quartets (512 items), totaling 3,328 items per arm. Paired queries keep tables fixed and ask the other row; quartet variants swap F assignments and independently remap G outputs, yielding four distinct correct answers. Save every token sequence, exact family IDs, answer, model prediction, candidate membership, and both alignment-head predictions when available. Score item accuracy, query-reversal pair exact, prediction changes, and factorial quartet exact with descriptive Wilson intervals. The same suite and seeds apply to every arm. Holdouts are unseen *families*, not unseen tokens, and do not guarantee semantic generalization.

## Frozen arms and interventions

Use the same native causal transformer family (RoPE, RMSNorm, gated MLP, zero dropout, AdamW `lr=3e-4`, weight decay `.01`, gradient clip `1.0`) and the same training mixture/schedule as TEACH-0002: steps 0–1499 first-hop/direct/copy 45/45/10%; 1500–2999 first-hop/direct/composed/copy 30/30/30/10%; 3000–4999 10/20/60/10%. Each arm gets **5,000 updates, batch 64**, streaming **320,000 example presentations**. For each of two initialization/stream seeds, all five arms see the same input episodes at every step and start from a seeded initialization for their architecture. The five arms are:

| Arm | Backbone | Extra target |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `dense_small` | 4 blocks, width 128, 4 heads, FF 256; about 0.67M parameters | none |
| `dense_medium` | 6 blocks, width 384, 6 heads, FF 1536; about 14.2M | none |
| `dense_large` | 8 blocks, width 768, 12 heads, FF 3072; about 75.6M, over 100× TEACH-0002 | none |
| `align_true` | **Identical large backbone and two learned two-row heads** | first-table and second-table row-index cross entropy, coefficient .5 |
| `align_random` | **Identical large backbone and heads** | independent uniform row labels, same coefficient .5 |

All arms instantiate the two small row heads so model capacity is exactly matched within the large-model contrast. The heads read the final answer-position hidden state. On first-hop episodes the F head is taught which of two name rows is queried; on direct episodes the G head is taught which key row is queried; on composed episodes both heads receive their true row indices. These auxiliary labels are derived from the training episode, never supplied at inference. `align_random` receives independently sampled row labels for exactly the same inputs. The answer loss and legal output vocabularies are unchanged in all arms. This tests whether *alignment supervision* helps the large model, not whether a built-in resolver can solve the task. A learned head may become accurate without the answer computation using it; that would not establish causal mediation.

The dense sweep is **exposure-matched** but necessarily uses different FLOPs across sizes. The **auxiliary-supervision** contrast at large size is matched in backbone, input stream, update count and head size, with only a small auxiliary-backprop cost difference. All models still predict answers through the same dense transformer output; the row heads do not route information into that answer at inference. Report measured wall time and estimated FLOPs separately; do not call a large-versus-small gain a pure parameter-only causal effect. No validation-selected checkpoint or post-hoc seed replacement is permitted.

## Decision gates fixed before seeing outcomes

Report each seed and arm separately. A numerical failure, partial arm, failed source/benchmark qualification, or resource stop is **INCOMPLETE**. Otherwise compute two separate, restrictive outcomes. `DENSE-SCALE-QUALIFIED` requires `dense_large` in **both seeds** to pass first-hop and direct held-out item accuracy ≥.90, both held-out query-pair exact rates ≥.80, both-held composed accuracy ≥.85, crossed composed cells each ≥.85, factorial quartet exact ≥.65, and copy ≥.95; its jointly held-out composed and factorial exact rates must each exceed `dense_small` by ≥.20. `ALIGNMENT-QUALIFIED` applies the same behavioral gates to `align_true`, additionally requires held-out F and G row-head accuracy ≥.90 on their single-table tasks, paired row-head exact ≥.80, **both** F and G row-head accuracy ≥.90 on jointly held-out composed items, and ≥.15 absolute gain in jointly held-out composed and factorial quartet exact over **both** `dense_large` and `align_random`, in **both seeds**. The two claims are independent: either, both or neither may qualify. All thresholds use exact denominators and raw proportions, not cherry-picked examples or confidence-interval overlap.

If neither passes, report **NOT QUALIFIED**; a failed treatment does not prove scaling or learned addressing cannot work in general. Even a qualified synthetic teacher would need a newly registered, fresh-family activation/parameter intervention study before any mechanistic claim, and separate manuscript-generator discrimination before a Voynich claim. [PROTOCOL.md](../research/PROTOCOL.md) remains the evidence standard.
