# EXP-0038 result — word-boundary prediction survives four transcription views

**Adaptive exploratory outcome: both registered labels are true; independent source-to-score audit passed.** On the already exposed ZL3b validation leaves, the last unit of one word predicts the first unit of the next word better than an edge-role/section/hand baseline, the previous word's first unit, or its length under the registered train-fitted categorical models. The effect survives two EVA compound segmentations and exclusion of loci with uncertain space, alternative reading, rare EVA, unreadable text or unknown spans. These four views all derive from the *same* transcription. They are a sensitivity check, not independent historical confirmation.

**Prior-art correction, added after scoring on 2026-09-25.** The qualitative end-of-token→start-of-next-token dependence was previously reported by [Currier (1976), as summarized by Reddy and Knight](https://aclanthology.org/W11-1511.pdf), directly tested for boundary-glyph combinations by [Smith and Ponzi (2019)](https://agnosticvoynich.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/06/glyph-combinations-across-word-breaks-in-the-voynich-manuscript-preprint.pdf), and quantified with matched prose, cipher and self-citation controls by [Rozanova and Temerev (2026)](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.17096v1). Reddy and Knight also found weak *whole-token* order but stronger edge-character predictability. The recent study reports about 0.2 bits of shuffle-corrected edge-glyph information; that is a different estimator and data selection from the held-out predictive bits/pair below, so numerical agreement should not be asserted. EXP-0036–0038 reproduce and further stress-test a **known phenomenon** under this project's split, endpoint/half-shuffle, uncertainty and train-only section/hand controls. The registration's prior-work review missed the two directly on-point boundary studies and should not be read as a novelty claim. This correction changes interpretation, not any registered metric, threshold, source or score.

**Interpretation and limit.** Under this predictor family, the previous transcribed word's terminal unit contains local ordering information that section/hand/edge-role, its initial unit and its length do not explain. The result is a constraint for any proposed manuscript generator. It does **not** show that the units are true glyphs, that spaces are linguistic word breaks, that the author copied words, that the direction of reading is backward, that a null layer exists, or that any word has a meaning. Natural-language morphology/syntax, a stateful cipher, finer layout rules and nonsemantic local generation all remain possible. The fixed backoff strengths (50/20) and one exposed validation split do not exhaust no-copy predictors. EXP-0028's negative one-edit word-cache result is unchanged. A stronger next study should compare richer context/word-identity no-copy baselines and explicit competing generators, then freeze a test on *independent* transcription or genuinely fresh physical leaves before any historical mechanism claim.
