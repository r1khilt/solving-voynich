# EXP-0032 — large, capacity-matched recurrence encoder on a new Polish source

**Registered before any EXP-0032 model training or Polish model score.** Exploratory synthetic recoverability and architecture comparison, not a Voynich decipherment or a claim that its historical glyph identities are arbitrary.

## Question and prior work

EXP-0030's corrected-channel 70,301-parameter BiLSTM failed copy-mutate recovery; EXP-0031 found that arbitrary synthetic glyph renaming flips 24.71% of its copy-row decisions without changing gold masks or copy-aware features. Does removing that *provable synthetic nuisance* improve null localization when model capacity and data are matched at a much larger scale, and does it transfer to an unseen source/language? The null channel remains a hypothesis, not a discovered manuscript property.

[Kambhatla et al. (2023)](https://aclanthology.org/2023.findings-eacl.160/) use symbol recurrence encoding in supervised substitution decipherment, including homophonic ciphers; their plaintext prediction and known target-language setting differ from mixed insertion/deletion over an unknown source. [Cohen and Welling (2016)](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v48/cohenc16.html) motivate hard symmetry constraints; the applicable group here is arbitrary renaming of the **synthetic** cipher pool, not spatial translation or an asserted Voynich symmetry. [ALICE v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.07282v2) demonstrates a different learned substitution decoder but does not justify a bijective output key for our homophone/merge/null generator. Prior Voynich applications and limitations are reviewed in `docs/research/deep-review-2026-09-21/DECIPHERMENT.md`; especially EXP-0011/0014b/0016b/0023/0024/0026 copy scores have the CHANNEL-ALIGNMENT-0001 erratum. This experiment uses the **corrected** generator and separately replays its true pre-null source.

This is source/language-disjoint from *this* training pair, though project-level prior exploration and genre mismatch keep the claim exploratory. No holdout metric is used for checkpoint selection.

A mathematically unrecoverable negative control uses 600 rows seed 320035: choose a 40-glyph alphabet, draw all 128 visible glyphs iid uniform, and independently choose exactly 90 keep positions uniformly. Conditional on the visible string, all `C(128,90)` masks have equal likelihood. A model exceeding a matched exact-count random decoder substantially on this control would indicate leakage, metric error or chance and invalidates a simple recovery claim. The positive competence control is the trained periodic filler family on Polish; its deterministic phase structure offers observable information. It is a *mechanism-specific* optimizer check, not proof of copy recovery.

## Matched architecture and training

Two encodings feed **the same** 6-layer, 8-head, width-384, feedforward-1536 bidirectional Transformer with a 128-position embedding, four copy-aware features, mask head and world head; both have **10,746,629** trainable parameters, over 150× the failed small model. The `raw` condition sees actual glyph IDs. The `canonical` condition replaces each synthetic cipher-pool glyph with its within-window first-occurrence index while keeping Latin cleartext letters and spaces literal. This exactly preserves equality/repetition and is invariant to arbitrary pool renaming; it deliberately discards glyph-shape identity. Both conditions use identical 120-entry vocab size, feature tensors, masks, initialization seed, source windows, architecture, optimizer and update count. They differ only in input IDs. Context-wide self-attention can combine repeated-symbol relations across the window; success at this scale is still extrapolation from the cited substitution work, not guaranteed on a mixed null channel.

Model seeds **320101 and 320202** per condition; four local MPS runs. For each: AdamW learning rate 3e-4, weight decay 1e-4, batch32 with 22 WORLD_C +10 other-world rows, null-weighted mask BCE (null weight2) +0.10 world cross-entropy, gradient clip1, dropout0.1, **3,000** updates. Every250 updates choose the checkpoint with highest validation **copy-mutate null F1** under a fixed exact-count decoder retaining the top90/128 keep probabilities; ties retain the earlier checkpoint. No Polish data are loaded by the training code. Pre-score 12-step MPS probe: 10,744,321 parameters in the probe, 0.0926s/update, ~1.2 GB allocated; four 3,000-step runs estimate roughly20–30 minutes including data/validation, with **600-second hard cap per run** and a separate 300-second evaluation cap. No paid API and no open-ended training.

## Fixed comparisons and decisions

After all four checkpoints and training reports exist and are hash-linked, run Polish and iid evaluation once. The same exact-count top90/128 decoder applies to all. For each Polish family report micro null precision/recall/F1, prefix-and-length-penalized `recon_accuracy`, and exact-mask rate. Report model-seed variation, validation selected steps, and 20-seed matched exact-count random controls (seeds 320400–320419). For the 198 Polish copy rows, average the two **paired canonical-minus-raw micro null-F1 gains**; resample rows 2,000 times with shared indices for all four models (seed 320320) to get a 95% interval. The models' raw probability arrays remain ignored; compact predicted masks and fixed seven-row probability probes are saved for separate full arithmetic/direct-inference audit.

The result can support an architecture benefit only if **all** of these frozen conditions hold: (1) both variants in both seeds score Polish periodic null F1 ≥0.70 (positive competence); (2) each model's iid-control null F1 is no more than matched-random mean +0.05; (3) canonical encoding and output pass exact renaming control, probability max error ≤1e-6; (4) paired mean copy null-F1 gain ≥0.05 and row-bootstrap lower bound >0.02; (5) mean canonical copy reconstruction exceeds the Polish copy matched-random mean by >0.05. If a competence, negative or invariance control fails, classify the architecture comparison **inconclusive/invalid**. If controls pass but benefit gates fail, record **NOT SUPPORTED**. Do not call a synthetic PASS decipherment. No threshold may be changed after seeing Polish or iid model scores. A separate auditor must reconstruct every saved mask's metrics, all random baselines and the paired interval and replay the frozen raw probability probes before any interpretation.
