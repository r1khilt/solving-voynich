# EXP-0041 result — four readable works and simple reversible encodings stay below the manuscript edge score

**Audited adaptive calibration; no decipherment.** Under the unchanged EXP-0039 train-fitted boundary scorer, the observed matched ZL validation manuscript has a previous-last-glyph real-minus-half-shuffle contrast of **+0.19717 bits/pair**. Four fixed windows of readable text wrapped into the same run/context template range from **+0.02373 to +0.09701**. Eight reversible two-homophone English encodings range **+0.06304 to +0.09183**; eight reversible within-word transpositions range **−0.00529 to +0.02488**. A one-to-one alphabet substitution gives exactly the same scores as English under paired shuffles, as mathematically expected. None of these 21 views matches the manuscript edge value, but four works and two simple channel families are far too narrow to rule out language, encipherment, other tokenization or a more complex process.

## What it does and does not change

These controls make the unusually high manuscript edge contrast harder to dismiss as a generic consequence of ordinary adjacent words, simple substitution, two-way homophony, or within-word shuffling **in these source windows under this estimator**. Their mixed lexical profiles show why one statistic cannot identify a generating mechanism: Finnish and Italian have manuscript-scale singleton fractions yet much weaker edge contrasts; homophony and transposition create too many new word forms while weakening the edge contrast. This is consistent with the broader conclusion of [Rozanova and Temerev (2026)](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.17096v1) under their different corpus/estimator, but it is not a direct numerical replication.

The source windows are one English novel, one Spanish novel, and two poems, not a representative sample of medieval prose, abbreviations, registers or all languages. The raw-book folding still collapses diacritic contrasts and splits apostrophes; artificial Voynich run wrapping destroys the books' original lineation and assigns manuscript section/hand metadata unrelated to their content. The four plaintext and cipher views use different glyph alphabets and novelty regimes from the manuscript, so their score magnitudes cannot become a universal historical model ranking. The manuscript's nine validation leaves are repeatedly exposed; no independent physical test was opened. A more powerful no-copy contextual predictor and explicit stateful, verbose and homophonic channels calibrated on multiple readable source families are needed before historical hypotheses can be narrowed further. No word meaning, language, key or plaintext was recovered.
