# EXP-0010 — Longer context with matched target exposure and locus-boundary controls

Registered 2026-09-21 before manuscript fitting. This is a bounded exploratory continuation of EXP-0005/0007, run alongside EXP-0008/0009 under CAMPAIGN-0001. Source and registration must be committed and pushed before execution. The hardware-only timing probe described below used artificial uniform token IDs on uncommitted source; it was not a manuscript experiment.

## Question and prior evidence

Does allowing substantially more preceding transcription text improve prediction when model capacity, initial weights, sampled training targets, update count and evaluation coordinates are controlled? Does the identity of the transcription's locus-separator token contribute? These questions relate to the context/layout hypotheses in the hypothesis register; neither is a decipherment test.

Reviewed the repository's prior Voynich neural/cryptographic work and architecture review, then primary sources before implementation:

- [Reddy and Knight, 2011](https://aclanthology.org/W11-1511.pdf), sections 5–6, compares local versus distant correlations and page-specific distributions. This motivates separating usable context from page composition. Their Currier transcription and selected B-language sections differ from our all-section ZL3b preparation; their historical interpretations are not assumed correct.
- [Dai et al., Transformer-XL, 2019, version 3](https://arxiv.org/html/1901.02860v3) discusses context fragmentation and mechanisms for longer dependencies. We retain our existing dense transformer and RoPE to isolate context length; we do not implement segment recurrence or claim to replicate Transformer-XL.
- [Muennighoff et al., 2023, version 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.16264v2), introduction and data-constrained setup, demonstrates diminishing returns from repeated data at much larger scales. This motivates recording exact repeated target exposure and fixed budgets. Its fitted scaling coefficients are not extrapolated to Voynich.
- [Shen and Smith, ALICE, 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.07282) uses supervised substitution-cipher learning and an explicit mapping constraint. Its assumptions and plaintext supervision differ from our ciphertext-like next-unit prediction; longer context alone does not provide a decoder or a cipher-family identification method.

The existing compact model already improves over local counts, and EXP-0005/0007 motivate recency and boundary controls. These are reasons to test additional history, not evidence that an optimal historical code span is 2,048 units.

## Data, representations and frozen evaluation coordinates

Use the unchanged registered ZL3b corpus, train-only 112-entry EVA tokenizer and physical-leaf split: train 177 pages, validation 24, final test 25. `corpus_identity` verifies all corpus digests against the preparation manifest; test bytes are hashed for provenance only. No final-test text is decoded, selected or scored. Uncertain/unknown targets remain masked under the established tokenizer policy. Same-page contexts include available BOS but never preceding pages. EVA units are transcription units, not verified individual glyphs or plaintext characters.

Training source blocks are the 209 fixed, nonoverlapping, right-padded 2,048-position page windows from the training split. They contain 181,868 scorable target positions and 428,032 allocated slots in total. Sample four source blocks uniformly with replacement per optimizer update. For shorter-context conditions, reshape those exact blocks into 256/512/1,024-unit pieces. Thus, for a given seed and completed update count, every condition receives the **same actual target positions, uncertainty masks and exposure counts**. Context resets differ by design. This changes window sampling relative to older experiments, so their absolute validation scores and training curves are not directly comparable.

Primary validation coordinates: seed 10103 samples 16 eligible targets per validation page, with at least 64 preceding units and no EOS/uncertainty target. This yields 384 targets, 24 pages and 10 leaf groups. Each condition gets `min(context_length, available_same_page_history)` preceding units; missing history is never filled with other pages. Only 24 of these 384 targets have a full 2,048-unit prefix. Mean available lengths are 225.15, 341.73, 446.40 and 542.61 units for the four maximum lengths.

Secondary long-prefix coordinates: independently sample up to 32 eligible targets per page that have at least 2,048 preceding units, with the same RNG stream. This yields 128 targets from **four pages / two leaf groups**: f76r, f76v, f111r and f111v. These cannot establish manuscript-wide long-range structure. The primary/secondary union has 512 distinct coordinates for this fixed draw. Coordinates and coverage are recorded before model scoring. Do not choose a favorable page subset after results.

The representation condition replaces every `<line>` input ID with `<space>` **without changing positions, sequence lengths, targets, output alphabet or scoring masks**. Targets still distinguish original spaces and locus separators. It is intentionally an input-information ablation. It tests whether knowing previous locus-boundary identity helps prediction of the original transcription stream. It is not a generative model of a newly flattened transcription.

`<line>` joins transcription loci, which can include labels and nonstandard text arrangements. This experiment makes no uniform physical-line, paragraph-reset, or scribe claim. No geometry is inferred from newline alone. Grouped scores for glyph, space and locus-separator targets reveal whether a result is confined to predicting formatting.

## Conditions and optimization

Twenty-one independently fitted models, three seeds 10111/10112/10113:

| Backbone | Context | Representation | Runs |
| --- | --- | --- | ---: |
| Existing main: width 192, four layers, four heads, SwiGLU 512; 1,814,208 parameters | 256, 512, 1,024, 2,048 | Original | 12 |
| Existing compact: width 128, two layers, four heads, SwiGLU 352; 430,720 parameters | 256, 2,048 | Original | 6 |
| Existing main | 2,048 | Merge locus-boundary input identity with spaces | 3 |

All use the existing dense causal model, dropout 0.1, AdamW with betas (0.9, 0.95), 0.1 weight decay on matrices only, global gradient clip 1, float32, no auxiliary loss or pretrained weights. Same-capacity models share identical initial-weight digests across contexts/representations for a given seed. Sampled-block RNG is separate from the model RNG; its index stream is hashed to audit exposure matching. Dropout draws can differ as batch shapes change.

Fixed 1,200 optimizer updates, four shared source blocks/update, at most 8,192 scored targets/update before masking/padding, microbatches capped at 2,048 input positions, and accumulation weighted by **one global valid-target count**. The actual expected count is about 3,481 scorable targets/update because many pages are shorter than 2,048. Wholly unscored rows are skipped without changing the objective. Learning rate 0.0006, 100-update linear warmup, cosine decay to 10% of the initial rate. Evaluation at initialization and each 200 updates. No validation-patience stopping; common endpoint comparisons require all 1,200 updates. A validation-selected checkpoint is saved for future analysis, but the primary registered contrast uses the fixed final checkpoint, avoiding a different stopping point per condition. Initialization, best and last checkpoints remain local and ignored.

Run order rotates the seven conditions across the three seeds. Each run is sequential within this track; the other campaign tracks may share the GPU. Report actual optimizer updates, scored target exposures, executed input slots, per-layer-independent attention pair slots, parameter counts and wall time. Attention-pair slots are a shape-based work proxy, not a measured FLOP count. Token exposure is matched; compute is **not** matched. Concurrent timing is not an isolated throughput comparison.

## Baselines, endpoint controls and decision rules

Fit unchanged train-only unigram and smoothed five-gram counts. Additional fixed baselines mix five-gram prediction 50/50 with a prefix symbol distribution, smoothed by 32 unigram pseudo-observations. Compare uniform weighting with exponential recency time constants 64, 256 and 1,024; all use at most 2,048 available prefix units. No coefficient is selected using validation. These are simple frequency explanations, not replicas of neural cache architectures.

At each completed longer-context run's final checkpoint, score the same coordinates with (a) normal context, (b) a cap of 256 preceding units and (c) the portion older than the latest 256 shuffled with coordinate-deterministic seed 10104. The suffix, length and inventory are controlled. Report these as distribution-shift interventions on a frozen model; shuffling can alter boundaries and group forms jointly.

Primary metric: average next-unit loss in bits on the 384 common validation coordinates. Primary contrasts are each main longer-context condition versus main256; compact2048 versus compact256; and boundary-merged main2048 versus original main2048. Report all three seed effects. Report pooled/equal-leaf means and a descriptive 2,000-draw leaf bootstrap (seed 10105) for the primary sample. These intervals do not adjust for multiple comparisons and are not a final confirmatory test.

A longer-context result is a **practical follow-up signal**, not a historical claim, only if all three final-checkpoint paired primary gains are positive, their mean is at least 0.02 bits/unit, and each longer-context model also performs better with its normal context than with its own cap256. Failure is reported. Compact and main comparisons remain separate. Report boundary information loss directly rather than inventing a new success threshold after seeing it. For the 128-target long-prefix subgroup, report per-page/per-seed effects and means; with only two leaves, do not use a bootstrap interval to imply generalization.

Primary conditions use development pages already consulted in prior research. The result can guide new work but cannot serve as an untouched manuscript benchmark, a semantic claim, or a historical attribution.
