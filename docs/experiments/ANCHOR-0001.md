# ANCHOR-0001: cross-illustration label-to-herbal lexical transfer

**Question, registered before label scoring.** Five published visual correspondences link numbered pharmaceutical plant fragments to full herbal drawings. Each numbered fragment has an `Lf` label in the ZL3b transcription. If a label acts like a stable plant name or specific descriptor, does it resemble a word on its matching herbal page more than the other fragment labels on the *same pharmaceutical page*? This is an exploratory cross-modal identity test, not an attempt to read the label into a known language.

## Source and eligibility

- The five exact pairs, `Lf` locus IDs and Yale IIIF canvas identifiers are fixed in `data/manifests/anchor0001_pairs.json`. The image correspondences are published assessments by [Zandbergen, Quire 15](https://www.voynich.nu/q15/index.html) and [Quire 19](https://www.voynich.nu/q19/index.html); they are hypotheses with visual support, not guaranteed botanical identity. [Beinecke's manuscript record](https://beinecke.library.yale.edu/beinecke/collections/beinecke-cipher-voynich-manuscript) is the source of official scans. The downloaded scan metadata is SHA-bound in the pair manifest; bulk images remain ignored. Fragment #54's label ID is inferred from the five row-1 labels' order because ZL3b omits its editorial number; the other four IDs have explicit numeric comments. Report a four-direct-label sensitivity analysis with the same scores and no refitting.
- [Zandbergen's illustration survey](https://www.voynich.nu/illustr.html) notes earlier observers of herbal/pharmaceutical copies and warns that plant identifications are uncertain. His [label survey](https://www.voynich.nu/extra/labels.html) finds pharmaceutical labels differ from running text; resemblance alone need not name a plant. [Hromada's calendar-name fitting](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.05381) illustrates the danger of a flexible name-list fitness function without an independent final decoder. [Knight et al.'s decipherment framework](https://aclanthology.org/P06-2065/) makes the source/channel assumptions explicit; here we test only a narrow lexical-transfer implication, not a plaintext channel.
- Use only `data/processed/zl3b/train.jsonl` and `validation.jsonl` with the pinned hashes from `data/manifests/zl3b_preparation.json`. Never open `test.jsonl` in the runner. The two f89v2 pairs are on the already-exposed validation leaf f89; the other three are on train leaves. All certain `Lf` labels on each pharmaceutical source page serve as within-page controls. The published `<!N>` in IVTFF is an editorial fragment locator, not a manuscript glyph. An eligible query is the exact predeclared `Lf` locus ID with certain EVA text matching `[a-z']+`. A true pair with ambiguous/missing label is reported as ineligible and the support gate fails; no replacement.

## Fixed readout and decision

For each of five pairs, collect certain `[a-z']+` words from `P0` loci on its matched herbal page. For each eligible `Lf` label on the same pharmaceutical page, score the herbal page by `1 - min_herbal_word Levenshtein(label, word) / max(len(label), len(word))`. This is a deliberately simple orthographic transfer score; it is not cosine semantics, and its value can be high for formulaic spelling alone. Rank the true fragment label against all eligible same-page `Lf` labels with midranks for ties. Convert to a rank fraction from 0 (worst) to 1 (best); the primary statistic is the mean of all five fractions. Exact identity anywhere on the herbal page is a separately reported secondary observation.

Enumerate all injective assignments of the known herbal pages to eligible labels *within each pharmaceutical page*, preserving the two-pair coupling on f89v2 and f102r2. The exact one-sided randomization probability is the proportion of assignments whose mean rank fraction is at least the observed mean. Report individual ranks, candidate counts, source-page group contributions and the complete null distribution summary. Repeat the descriptive rank summary for the four pairs with direct numeric label comments, but do not call this a separate significance test. No adaptive choice of edit metric, locus type, first-line restriction or pair exclusion after scores.

The registered `lexical_transfer_candidate` gate requires all five true labels eligible, mean rank fraction at least 0.75, exact randomization probability at most 0.05, and at least four of five true labels at or above fraction 0.5. If the gate fails, the decision is `not_supported_in_this_small_panel`. Even a pass would mean only that these five independently proposed image correspondences carry reproducible **orthographic transfer** under this assay. It would not establish a plant-name reading, a language, or full decipherment. Treat the three pharmaceutical source pages, not five pairs, as the main dependence units; the randomization probability is conditional on these pages and should not be generalized as a corpus-wide p-value.

## Reproducibility and limits

Freeze source and this registration before readout. Add a synthetic assignment/rank test and an independent input/score/decision audit. Record exact hashes for script, pair manifest, corpus and result; no paid API, no model training, CPU target under five minutes and compact result under 1 MiB. Keep raw Yale images ignored. The unusually small high-confidence set is the chief power limit. A negative result cannot reject visual referents if herbal paragraphs omit names, labels name plant parts, or the script uses inflection/abbreviation beyond one edit. A positive result must be replicated on a larger image-matched set selected without examining text.
