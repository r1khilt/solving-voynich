# EXP-0041 — readable-language and reversible-cipher calibration of the edge assay

**Registered before any EXP-0041 comparator score, 2026-09-25 PDT.** This is an adaptive exploratory calibration of the already exposed EXP-0039 ZL validation assay. It is not a language-identification contest, a new Voynich final test, or a decipherment claim.

## Why this comparison

[Reddy and Knight (2011)](https://aclanthology.org/W11-1511/) document unusual Voynich word structure and earlier edge observations. [Hauer and Kondrak (2016)](https://aclanthology.org/Q16-1006/) show strong language identification and recovery on *specified synthetic monoalphabetic/anagram channels*, then give a conditional Hebrew suggestion for Voynich; a compatible-looking score does not validate their channel. [Rozanova and Temerev (2026)](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.17096v1) already compare Voynich with several natural-language, verbose-cipher and pseudo-text controls on matched token counts; their Latin narrative has edge information 0.033 versus Voynich 0.197 under **their** plug-in shuffle-corrected mutual-information estimator, and their Naibbe cipher has 0.006. They also show simple substitution preserves appropriate information quantities, but verbose/homophonic changes need not. EXP-0041 is not a rediscovery of their comparison. It asks whether our *train-fitted, held-out, role/context-adjusted log-score* behaves sensibly on independent readable and known-key channel controls, after EXP-0040 used that exact family of scores to challenge five nonsemantic generators. The estimators, corpora, sample size and line treatments differ, so the two papers' numeric scores must not be equated with ours.

The natural-language source texts are the locally cached raw Project Gutenberg [Alice's Adventures in Wonderland](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/11), [Kalevala](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/7000), [La Divina Commedia](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/1000), and [Don Quijote](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/2000), previously acquired by `src/voynich/multilang_recovery.py`. A pre-score inspection found the older cached `*.romanized.txt` had incorrectly split some accented letters into new words (for example Finnish `lähteäni`); those files are **not** used. Instead, decode each SHA-pinned raw UTF-8 file, apply Unicode NFKD, remove combining marks, lowercase and extract `[a-z]+` word runs. This still folds language distinctions and splits apostrophes/hyphens. These are prose and verse controls, **not** a representative sample of medieval manuscript genres or source languages.

From each anchor take exactly 19,071 consecutive training words followed immediately by 2,685 validation words. Require `[a-z]+` for every selected token and fail on mismatch. Any editorial intrusion or source discontinuity discovered afterward is a recorded limitation, not a reason to silently move the offset. Wrap each sequence into the 2,697 train / 350 validation groups in the manuscript template order; this breaks natural source lines at artificial locations but fixes sample size, run lengths, pseudo-context assignment and shuffle opportunities. Report source-window SHA, alphabet size, lexical novelty and singleton share. Treat template leaf bootstrap intervals, if computed, as invalid for book-to-book generalization: the source windows are one work each.

On the **English** control only, also apply (a) one global random bijection of the 26 letters with seed `410100`, (b) eight independently keyed two-homophone substitutions (seeds `410200`–`410207`): each plaintext letter maps to a unique atomic symbol `letter+0/1`, with choice determined by a keyed per-token pseudorandom generator; the decoder discards the variant, and (c) eight keyed within-word transpositions (seeds `410300`–`410307`), each using a per-token pseudorandom permutation with its inverse. Every channel must reconstruct its entire 21,756-word source exactly before it may be scored. The first channel should preserve scores to floating tolerance when both train and validation are relabeled and the estimator is refit; if this positive invariance check fails, the comparison is invalid. The other channels test sensitivity, not likely Voynich mechanisms. No channel is fitted to manuscript measurements.

## Scorer and interpretation fixed in advance

For each view, fit the unchanged EXP-0039 smoothed target-initial-glyph model to only that view's wrapped training groups; score its wrapped validation groups. Repeat the unchanged endpoint-fixed, interior-half-preserving shuffle **200** times with seed `410400 + 1000 × view_index`, where view order is English, Finnish, Italian, Spanish, bijection, eight homophones in seed order, eight transpositions in seed order. Primary descriptive quantity: real-minus-mean-shuffle previous-last gain in bits per pair; also report absolute last gain, last-minus-first, full-identity incremental gain, lexical novelty and singleton share. The manuscript reference is read unchanged from `results/EXP-0039/results.json`, SHA-256 to be verified by the runner. No thresholds are tuned to any EXP-0041 control outcome. An interpreter may say the edge score is *not specific to Voynich* if a known readable/control stream reaches or exceeds the manuscript reference under this scorer, but cannot infer a source language or cipher from similarity. Conversely, failure of four texts and two simple transforms does not exclude natural language or ciphers generally.

An independent script must reconstruct the source windows and channels, replay all view scores and all 4,200 shuffles using the separately written EXP-0039 auditor, and verify the result JSON. Fixed CPU wall cap: 180 seconds each for runner and auditor; no paid API, download, or neural training. Run focused unit controls, Ruff, compilation, and repository tests before scoring. Commit and push this registration/code/tests/notebook and verify the remote ref **before** the first EXP-0041 score. After the run, report all controls, including failures and source-text limitations; preserve final-test leaves unopened.
