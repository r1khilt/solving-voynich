# EXP-0034 result — the known synthetic channel makes source-given nulls highly localizable

**Registered descriptive gain rule passes, with a post-result numerical-tie audit amendment.** On the 198 exposed Polish copy-mutate rows, a source-conditioned oracle weighted by the corrected generator reaches mean null F1 **0.9662**, versus **0.9330** for EXP-0033's uniform string-alignment rule: paired gain **+0.03320**, row-bootstrap 95% interval **[+0.02903,+0.03721]**. This clears the registered mean ≥0.02 and lower-bound >0 rule. It does **not** show that weighting alone causes the whole difference: EXP-0034 also receives the full pre-null source, its length, alphabet, copy-family identity and visible retained count, while the uniform baseline receives only the visible retained string. Neither receives this oracle information in a real Voynich decoding attempt.

The median posterior probability of the **particular saved gold mask** is **0.0763** even with all this oracle information. This is compatible with EXP-0033's finding that identical or copy-related characters create several feasible paths. It means a high per-position score can coexist with uncertainty about the exact historical insertion events in the synthetic generator. The weighted top-count mask exactly equals the saved gold mask in **36/198** rows; this exact-hit count was computed *after* the registered F1 result and is descriptive, not a selection gate. The paired F1 gain is positive in **147** rows, zero in **42**, and negative in **9**. The one numerical tie can change one row's F1 by0.02778, or the 198-row mean by0.0001403; it cannot explain the +0.033 gain.

## What this changes—and what it does not

EXP-0033's uniform alignment was a pessimistic description of the *known synthetic* copy channel. When given the full source, alphabet and channel law, the correct probability weighting plus extra source information narrows uncertainty enough to locate most inserted positions. The source-free large model's copy F1 near0.47–0.48 therefore should not be described as a fundamental limit of the generator, nor should its zero exact masks be the sole progress metric. But this oracle comparison cannot say whether the model mainly failed to infer the source, to learn the channel, to handle unknown alphabet/length, or to optimize its objective. It also cannot support a Voynich null hypothesis, cipher key, language identity, word reading or decipherment.

The next ambitious, falsifiable step is **joint source-and-channel inference** on a *new* synthetic source with a real held-out language/genre and negative controls. Compare source-given, family-given, and source-free conditions with the same channel mechanism, then freeze any proposed decoder before manuscript diagnostics. The exposed Polish rows cannot serve as a new blind test for tuning that decoder. Historical application requires independent evidence that an insertion/copy channel is appropriate and must beat nonsemantic, layout and no-null alternatives under held-out physical folios.
