# BORG-COMMON-001: bounded historical common-name pilot

Prospective producer and audit registration, 2026-10-10. **Exploratory historical hypothesis; confirmatory synthetic competence gate.** Preparation selects bytes and creates controls without fitting a source or scoring a key. No historical answer has been acquired or opened by this preparation. The root researcher owns the publication freeze, the one scientific producer, notebook updates and subsequent decisions.

## Question and claim boundary

Can one shared, injective mapping from 21 common transcription names to a known 23-letter Latin source recover new planted keys, then produce an independently checkable candidate for a real historical cipher? This is a capability test, not a proposed Voynich translation. The known Latin language is assistance. The 21 ASCII names are a **working hypothesis**, not a globally verified glyph segmentation or a guarantee that the full Borg cipher is injective. `M` and `m` remain different names. Failure is preserved; no homophones, new alphabet, changed subset, source tuning or extra restarts may be introduced in this run.

The [official Borg project page](https://www.su.se/english/research/research-catalogue/research-projects/d/decipherment-of-historical-manuscripts/the-borg-cipher) describes a 408-page manuscript, Latin with some Italian, a larger graphical inventory, cleartext material and catchwords. These descriptions motivate a tractable historical control; they do not verify our 21-name subset. Its published key, corrected plaintext and translation are forbidden inputs until a separately registered **post-seal** external-check phase. Two official link labels lead to the same transcription file; do not assume that file is an independent answer.

## Model, allocation and controls

Use a fixed positive normalized recursive Dirichlet **1–4-gram** source: unigram additive concentration .1 per letter; each higher-order conditional row backs off to its normalized suffix row with concentration16. This 16 follows the already completed source-only NAIBBE-003 selection; it is not chosen from Borg or the new controls. Prefix lengths1,2,3 and all subsequent 4-grams score each record independently. Full literal scores are compiled into NumPy integer-index arrays and repeated n-gram counts. This is not C++ code, a neural world model, a posterior sampler or an exact global optimizer.

A key injects 21 observed names into 23 letters. Two unobserved positions complete it to a 23-permutation. Swaps with those positions change which source letters are unused; they do **not** posit two verified additional Borg glyphs. Source probabilities remain normalized over all 23 letters, not renormalized over a chosen 21-letter subset. Every candidate receives the whole fixed-record score.

Fixed search: PCG64, **12 random restarts × at most8 complete coordinate sweeps**, 23 pivots per sweep and all23 swaps per pivot. Stop a restart after a sweep with no strict improvement >1e-10. Retain the current key on ties; choose the best endpoint by score then lexicographic completed key. Preserve all twelve endpoint keys and scores. No annealing, adaptive extension, beam tuning or cherry-picked restart.

Exactly **seven fits**: two Latin planted positives, their two within-record shuffles, Borg-Latin, Borg-English and a within-record Borg shuffle scored under Latin. Exactly **six transfer sets**: the four planted positive/null sets and the two unshuffled historical language candidates. Nulls preserve record lengths and letter counts but destroy order; wrong-language and shuffle scores are diagnostics, not established language-identification tests. There is no translation-quality gate based on their likelihood.

Key seeds96701/96709; positive search96721/96729; paired-null search96821/96829; historical search96741/96749/96757; historical shuffle96767. Every selected control is encrypted with one shared planted key. The producer fits ciphertext only, then writes **all seven candidates and all84 endpoints to an exclusive durable seal before reading control answers or decoding any transfer material**.

Synthetic competence passes only if **both** positive cases have transfer letter error≤1% and correct assignments≥95% among the rows occurring in transfer. Equal-length substitution makes the letter mismatch count exact here; it is not a general edit-distance algorithm. Report integers and failed cases individually. Historical status remains `UNASSESSED_NO_EXTERNAL_ANSWER` regardless of fluency or score. The future external-check phase must collate the sealed name→letter mappings against independently acquired published key/ink, account for exceptions and compare transfer text; no refitting before that comparison.

[Ravi & Knight2011](https://aclanthology.org/P11-1025.pdf), introduction and substitution sections, motivate language priors plus one type-level key shared across occurrences. Their inference machinery, dictionaries and English settings are not reproduced here. [Reddy & Knight2011](https://aclanthology.org/W11-1511.pdf), sections5–6 and8–9, motivate explicit boundary assumptions and caution about interpreting distributional matches as a decipherment. Neither paper establishes transfer of this pilot to Voynich.
