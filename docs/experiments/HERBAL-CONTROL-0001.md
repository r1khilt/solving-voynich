# HERBAL-CONTROL-0001: cross-manuscript visual identity calibration

**Question, registered before feature scores.** Can a fixed off-the-shelf image feature method recognize the *same named herbal chapter* when the drawing comes from a different manuscript? This is a known-answer visual-control assay, not a Voynich plant identification or decipherment test. A method that fails here is not allowed to promote a Voynich resemblance to a plant name.

## Source review and supervision

[Zandbergen's Voynich illustration survey](https://www.voynich.nu/illustr.html) explicitly treats botanical likenesses as speculative; he proposes a striking f35v comparison with BnF Latin 6823 f60r while saying it is not a direct copy. [Biblissima's BnF f60 catalog record](https://portail.biblissima.fr/en/ark%3A/43093/ifdata306264506a84685b0f7486f00d66e160db74366d) assigns the *lierre* (ivy) and oak descriptors to the right-hand drawing, while [another record](https://portail.biblissima.fr/fr/ark%3A/43093/ifdatafae5a7c306d2d45a1d2a6153df2401ab7ba937ab) separately identifies the left-hand *aunée* drawing on the same page. The page therefore supplies a particularly useful same-source decoy. These are catalog identities, not an identification of any Voynich plant.

[Kaoua et al. (ICDAR 2021)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.08109) evaluate illustration matching across six manuscripts, over 2,000 images and 1,200 correspondences; they find that ordinary visual similarities struggle when copies vary substantially. Their cycle-consistent gains motivate eventual multi-manuscript matching, but that paper's annotated data and tuned methods do not transfer automatically to the tiny, selectively chosen Voynich panel. The [Oxford 1516 project](https://www.robots.ox.ac.uk/~vgg/research/1516/) also uses visual search for historical illustrations, mainly printed woodblocks, a different copying regime. [Apple Vision feature prints](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/vision/vngenerateimagefeatureprintrequest) offer a fixed local baseline and built-in [distance](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/vision/vnfeatureprintobservation); they are a proprietary generic feature representation, not a trained botanical classifier or interpretable proof of identity.

The known answers are Wikimedia Commons' historical [*enula*](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:De_enula_in_Tractatus_de_herbis), [*edera nigra*](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:De_edera_nigra_in_Tractatus_de_herbis), and [*herba vitis*](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:De_herba_vitis_in_Tractatus_de_herbis) **chapter** categories, each represented once from BnF Latin 6823, BL Egerton 747, and Casanatense 459. These are a deliberately selected feasibility panel, not random plants or verified modern species. Two of BnF's categories share f60r, so drawing-only crops are mandatory. `scripts/herbal_control_0001_crops.py` fixes the nine rectangles and SHA-256 checksums in `data/manifests/herbal_control_0001_images.json`; source JPEGs and crop PNGs remain ignored. The 1024-pixel BnF source was unavailable directly from Gallica (HTTP 403), so the image is a Commons copy that credits BnF. The category pages are secondary crowd-curated chapter metadata, with the BnF f60 ivy/enula assignments independently cross-checked against Biblissima.

## Fixed test and controls

Use Apple Vision `VNGenerateImageFeaturePrintRequest` **revision 2** on the fixed 512-by-512 white-padded crops. For each ordered pair of distinct manuscripts, use the three drawings from one manuscript as queries and the three from the other as a gallery: 3 × 2 × 3 = **18 retrievals**. Choose the gallery image with the smallest `VNFeaturePrintObservation.computeDistance` value. Primary statistic is exact top-1 chapter accuracy, counting ties as failures. This tests cross-manuscript matching, not same-page scan recognition; no model fitting or query-specific metric choice.

Enumerate all 6³=216 independent permutations of the three chapter labels within each manuscript, keeping the visual nearest-neighbor outcomes fixed, for an exact one-sided label-randomization probability. Report the 18 outcomes, per ordered manuscript pair, error modes, distance margins, exact p, and an all-classes-per-manuscript aggregation. A method passes the *pilot feasibility gate* only if accuracy is at least 14/18, each of the six ordered manuscript-pair accuracies is at least 2/3, and p ≤ 0.05. This gate is intentionally demanding; a pass does not estimate general retrieval accuracy because source classes/crops were chosen after seeing the pages. A fail means **no model-derived f35v identity claim from this method**. An unscored duplicate-image sanity control should have distance zero or very close to zero; the method must give finite, symmetric pair distances.

After the known-answer gate is evaluated and frozen, an **exploratory** f35v drawing-only crop may be queried against the nine historical controls. It must be reported with the full gallery ranking, not only the ivy examples, and it cannot license reading f35v's reserved transcription or claiming a botanical identity. The f35v Yale scan has already been visually inspected in this source block; its text has not been transcribed or scored. No later text holdout may be called image-blind for f35v. Residual writing in some historical crops, different rendering styles, crowd-curated chapter categories, multiple plants in one page, only three classes/manuscripts, and potential generic-model training overlap are material limitations.

Implementation and run caps: local Swift/Apple Vision only, no paid API or training, target under five minutes and under 100 MiB compact output. Log macOS/Swift/Vision revision, exact commands, source/crop/result hashes, failures and any corrections in `NOTEBOOK.md`; do not alter these criteria after seeing scores without marking a new exploratory run.
