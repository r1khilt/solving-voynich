# Prior work: Voynich language models, neural decipherment, and causal analysis

Research pass: **2026-09-20 PDT / 2026-09-21 UTC**. This is a targeted primary-source review, not a systematic review or a replication. Results below are the original authors' reports unless explicitly marked as our assessment. None of the cited models was run by this project during this review.

## What this changes about the project

Training a small model from scratch on Voynich is already established practice. There are also prior attempts to inspect its predictions and saliency. Our useful contribution would be a reproducible path from manuscript evidence to **held-out behavioral performance, causally tested computations, and recovery tests with known hidden mechanisms**. A new architecture alone is not evidence of a new decipherment method.

There are three different tasks which must remain distinct:

1. **Voynich-only prediction:** learn the next transcription unit from ciphertext-like text alone. No plaintext target is available.
2. **Synthetic decipherment:** train with known generators, sometimes with paired plaintext/ciphertext. This supplies ground truth for testing methods; it is a separate training condition.
3. **Historically constrained decipherment:** fit an explicit encoding/language model, usually with a known-language corpus or other external anchor. Its assumptions must be independently defensible.

Our initial predictor can do the first task and support the second. Success at either does not establish the third.

## Related decipherment research

| Primary source | Mechanism, supervision, and result | What transfers to this project |

| **Hauer & Kondrak (TACL 2016)**, [Decoding Anagrammed Texts Written in an Unknown Language and Script](https://aclanthology.org/Q16-1006/), [paper §5](https://aclanthology.org/Q16-1006.pdf) | Language identification and decoding under monoalphabetic substitution plus within-word anagram assumptions. The paper reports 97% source-language identification across 380 languages and 93% mean word accuracy on **synthetic** ciphertexts. Its Voynich application suggests Hebrew but explicitly acknowledges that outputs may be artifacts of anagramming and language-model flexibility. | This was not an LLM trained only on Voynich, and its synthetic accuracy is not Voynich decipherment accuracy. Measure degrees of freedom, compare languages and null inputs, and prevent a powerful plaintext model from turning arbitrary material into plausible prose. |
| **Gomez et al. (ICLR 2018)**, [CipherGAN](https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.04883), [original implementation](https://github.com/Cohere-Labs-Community/CipherGAN) | CycleGAN-inspired mapping between **unpaired banks of ciphertext and plaintext**; demonstrations on shift and Vigenère ciphers. | Unpaired is not plaintext-free. A useful future comparison for latent mappings, but the assumed plaintext distribution and known cipher family provide substantial information unavailable to the Voynich-only predictor. |
| **Luo, Cao & Barzilay (ACL 2019)**, [Neural Decipherment via Minimum-Cost Flow](https://aclanthology.org/P19-1303/), [code](https://github.com/j-luo93/NeuroDecipher) | Character seq2seq plus constrained lexicon alignment; input includes nonparallel text in a **known related language**. Reports 67.3% correct Linear B cognate translations. Constraints include monotonic character correspondences and sparse word mappings. | A demonstration of identifying known linguistic correspondences, not newly solving Linear B or arbitrary unknown text. Explicit latent mappings and constraints are valuable; the existence of a suitable relative and cognate overlap must be tested rather than assumed. |
| **Luo et al. (TACL 2021)**, [Deciphering Undersegmented Ancient Scripts Using Phonetic Prior](https://aclanthology.org/2021.tacl-1.5/), [code](https://github.com/j-luo93/DecipherUnsegmented/) | Joint segmentation and cognate alignment with phonetic constraints. Evaluated on Gothic, Ugaritic, and Iberian; its language-closeness analysis does not find strong support for the proposed Iberian–Basque relationship. | Word boundaries and target-language choice can be modeled hypotheses. The phonetic prior still assumes a relationship between written symbols and speech; it is not automatically appropriate for verbose codes, nulls, or a nonlinguistic generator. A useful precedent for reporting negative results. |
| **Aldarrab & May (ACL 2021)**, [Can Sequence-to-Sequence Models Crack Substitution Ciphers?](https://aclanthology.org/2021.acl-long.561/), [code](https://github.com/NadaAldarrab/s2s-decipherment) | End-to-end multilingual neural decipherment of simple substitutions, trained on synthetic examples and tested on synthetic/historical ciphers, including noisy inputs. | Include noise and language uncertainty in synthetic controls. Lack of an explicitly provided language label does not imply that the model has no plaintext-language training. This is a solver/control model rather than the primary Voynich-only language model. |
| **Kambhatla, Born & Sarkar (EACL Findings 2023)**, [Decipherment as Regression](https://aclanthology.org/2023.findings-eacl.160/), [code](https://github.com/protonish/decipher_symbol_recurrence) | A 23M-parameter causal Transformer trained on synthetic ciphertext/plaintext sequences uses symbol-recurrence encoding to solve simple and homophonic substitutions. Attention-derived key recovery is measured against known keys. The experiments preserve word boundaries. | Strong precedent for repeated-symbol representations and latent-equivalence tests. Their output target contains plaintext; ours initially does not. Key-recovery scoring against ground truth is much stronger than visually compelling attention alone. Human correction in some out-of-domain historical examples must remain distinct from fully automatic outcomes. |
| **Shen & Smith (arXiv v1, 8 September 2025)**, [ALICE](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.07282v1), [code](https://github.com/al-jshen/alice) | Encoder Transformer; supervised cipher/plaintext pairs; symbol pooling; optional Gumbel–Sinkhorn bijective head. Main model: 85M parameters. Training uses a large quote corpus with random substitutions, preserving spaces/punctuation. The roughly 1,500 figure counts **distinct keys**, not total training examples. | Particularly useful synthetic positive control. A readable permutation head trades some accuracy for explicit mapping; dynamic embeddings did not improve the reported results. A bijection would prematurely assume a Voynich cipher family, so use it only in a separately labeled constrained solver. Layerwise decoding is informative but does not by itself prove the proposed algorithm is causally necessary. |
| **Li et al. (ACL Findings 2025)**, [CipherBank](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-acl.309/) | A 2,358-problem benchmark spanning nine algorithms, evaluating pretrained chat/reasoning models including DeepSeek-V3/R1 and OpenAI models. | Useful for understanding frontier models as research assistants or known-cipher solvers. It does not measure a tiny Voynich-only predictor or identify the manuscript's unknown generating process. Separate knowing an algorithm from inferring which algorithm applies. |

## Manuscript structure and non-neural controls

- [Reddy & Knight (2011)](https://aclanthology.org/W11-1511.pdf) include a two-state HMM that approximately recovers the Currier A/B vocabulary division. **Our implication:** finding section-like latent clusters is not a new semantic discovery, and simple models must be included before attributing that result to architecture.
- [Lindemann & Bowern (2020)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.14697) compare transcription systems, scribal partitions, hundreds of language samples, and historical texts when studying conditional character entropy. **Our implication:** preserve transcription provenance, evaluate alternative unit definitions, and distinguish within-word predictability from information carried across words.
- [Greshko's Naibbe cipher (2025)](https://github.com/greshko/naibbe-cipher/blob/main/README.md) is a deliberately constructed, reversible verbose homophonic cipher producing several Voynich-like statistics. It is not a decipherment of the manuscript. **Our implication:** a difficult positive control should combine recoverable plaintext with misleading surface morphology; resembling nonlanguage in one statistic does not establish absence of encoded content.

Synthetic negative controls should include IID and shuffled material, finite-state/template text, and copy/mutate generators. Positive controls should include substitution, homophony, context-dependent mappings, verbose encodings, and structured null insertion. Every generator must expose its actual latent state and decoding rules. Matching Voynich statistics is a control-design objective, not proof of historical identity. These proposed controls are our design recommendations, not reported experiments in this project.

## Mechanistic-interpretability lessons to apply

| Source | Established contribution and scope | Required practice here |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Olsson et al. (2022), In-context Learning and Induction Heads](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2022/in-context-learning-and-induction-heads/index.html) | Studies pattern-copying circuits in small Transformers with direct ablations; evidence for broader model claims is more indirect. | Test whether an apparent long-range feature is merely sequence copying. Include repeated-symbol and induction probes, copy/mutate controls, and training checkpoints. A copying circuit alone does not reveal what generated the manuscript. |
| [Zhang & Nanda (2023), Towards Best Practices of Activation Patching](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.16042) | Shows that corruption method and metric can materially change localization findings. | Save exact clean/corrupted inputs, intervention sites, and metrics. Use matched perturbations and multiple sensible metrics rather than selecting whichever plot supports a hypothesis. |
| [Heimersheim & Nanda (2024), How to use and interpret activation patching](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.15255) | Discusses the evidence supplied by patching and pitfalls in interpreting sufficiency, necessity, and metrics. | Patch activations, not only visualize them; separate restoration of behavior from a complete explanation. Check necessity and sufficiency where feasible, controls, seed stability, and behavior on held-out examples. |

Our architecture should expose per-layer residual streams, per-head outputs and attention patterns, MLP intermediates, embeddings, logits, and intervention hooks. Prefer an implementation in which intervention/no-intervention equivalence and causal masking can be tested directly. These are design requirements derived from the task and methods above; no paper establishes them as a sufficient route to Voynich decipherment.

## Concrete experimental consequences

1. **Fit an inspectable predictor, then earn complexity.** Compare dense small-model sizes against n-gram, local-context, and copy controls. Modern architecture ingredients remain hypotheses until matched experiments improve generalization or make a mechanism easier to identify.
2. **Split before learned preprocessing.** Freeze manuscript-unit assignments before tokenizer fitting or hyperparameter search. Keep alternate transcriptions of the same physical material together. Audit repeated passages and boundary-crossing windows; log exposure of validation and test sets.
3. **Keep the raw evidence richer than the default model view.** Preserve line/page IDs, uncertainty, labels, and metadata even if the first objective only consumes transcription units. Layout ablations can then distinguish local spelling from line-position effects.
4. **Compare losses in common units.** Perplexity is tokenizer-dependent. For a fixed representation use bits per predicted unit; across tokenizers also report a declared common underlying-text denominator. Do not infer an entropy rate from raw model loss without stating model approximation and estimator limitations.
5. **Separate behavioral and causal milestones.** First establish held-out improvement over baselines, then test a prespecified behavior with activation interventions. Finally measure latent-mechanism recovery on controls where the answer is known.
6. **Hold out mechanisms, not just keys.** Synthetic recovery must vary plaintext sources, alphabets, keys, generator parameters, corruption regimes, and entire mechanism families where relevant. Otherwise a classifier can identify artifacts of our generator implementation.
7. **Retain ambiguity as an outcome.** If two known generators produce observationally indistinguishable text, a successful method should admit non-identifiability instead of confidently inventing a unique origin.
