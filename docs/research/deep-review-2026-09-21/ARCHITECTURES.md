# Architecture research: transitions, memory, representation and scarce data

Research date: 2026-09-21. This is a primary-source review and a set of proposed decisions, not an experiment or an exhaustive survey. No training or paid research API call was made. The machine-readable companion, [architectures.sources.json](architectures.sources.json), records exact versions, authors, reading depth and limitations. `selected_sections` means only the listed sections were inspected, never a full-paper claim. Interpretability methods and identifiability are covered by companion reviews.

## What changes after the first review

Our next architectural comparison should test **which update rule can maintain the right uncertainty**, rather than chiefly scaling model size or context. This is a project inference from the papers below and our [completed campaign](../../experiments/CAMPAIGN-0001-results.md): blind groupings did not transfer to new synthetic keys, copy gains mainly concerned the next symbol, broad causal changes worked on toys but not the manuscript, and longer context did not reliably win under the registered budget.

The strongest concrete candidates are a small explicit probabilistic recurrent model, a nonlinear recurrent control, and a DeltaProduct-style transition control. A compact transformer remains an essential reference. Newer architecture papers make this comparison more interesting; none establishes a Voynich decoding method.

## Recurrence: the mathematical distinctions matter

The useful common form is `state[t] = A(input[t]) state[t-1] + write(input[t])`. The shape of `A` determines what can be carried forward. A diagonal decay can preserve or erase coordinates; a non-diagonal update can mix them. This compact equation is an explanatory abstraction, not a claim that every model below has identical nonlinearities or readout.

**A recurrent label does not guarantee general state tracking.** Merrill, Petty and Sabharwal's analysis covers bounded-depth architectures and specified arithmetic assumptions; its separation invokes the usual complexity-class conjecture. It does not prohibit arbitrary nonlinear RNNs or every later linear recurrence. Grazzi and colleagues show why eigenvalue restrictions matter: allowing negative eigenvalues enables parity-like behavior, while richer products support stronger finite-state constructions. Their broad expressivity result has potentially large depth; it is not a guarantee that gradient descent learns the rule. [A11](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.08819v3), [A22](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.12537v1).

**DeltaProduct is a meaningful small-model candidate.** Multiple *different* generalized Householder updates per observed symbol expand the transition family. Repeating the same key can collapse into a simpler update, so merely increasing iterations is insufficient. Its synthetic experiments test training lengths 3–40 and extrapolation through 256. That is closer to our causal questions than broad-language leaderboards, but supplies known operation/task structure rather than an unknown historical encoding. [A12](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.10297v3).

**Gated DeltaNet and Kimi improve memory management, with different mechanisms.** GDN combines targeted associative replacement with global decay. Kimi Delta Attention changes that scalar decay to coordinate-specific decay and retains periodic full attention. The Kimi paper has unusually relevant two-layer synthetic palindrome/recall/stack tests, but reports best training-accuracy curves after learning-rate search. Our successor must select on development data and confirm on fresh keys and lengths. GDN's 1.3B/100B-token comparison and Kimi's 48B-total/3B-active setup do not determine a winner for our approximately million-parameter models. [A08](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.06464v2), [A09](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.26692v1).

**Mamba-3 weakens any blanket claim that older SSM limitations describe every current SSM.** It adds richer discretization, complex rotations and multiple input/output channels. The paper evaluates language models from 180M to 1.5B trained on 100B tokens and explicitly still reports a transformer retrieval advantage. Improved state tracking is not equivalent to unrestricted transition expressivity or ciphertext-only mechanism recovery. Its kernels and latency measurements also do not establish MPS performance. [A10](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.15569v1).

**Partial observability is the critical harder case.** The February 2026 code-tracking paper distinguishes known deterministic actions from ambiguous transitions with occasional state reveals. Its belief update includes conditioning and renormalization, which a simple linear state update cannot automatically substitute for. A model may pass permutation tracking yet fail at maintaining uncertainty when the next operation is hidden. That closely motivates our branch/null control, while remaining an analogy rather than evidence about Voynich. [A24](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.14814v1).

Project proposal: compare explicit normalized beliefs, an ordinary nonlinear recurrent model, and a signed low-rank/product recurrence on **the same visible streams**. Include deterministic, ambiguous, copying, and IID cases. Score joint continuation likelihood and causal state-update consistency, not only marginal forecasts or clusters. Test new alphabets/keys, length changes, missing observations, and transitions absent from training. None of these successor experiments has been run.

## Test-time memory is adaptation, not free information

TTT makes the state itself a learned model updated by an inner self-supervised objective. Its minibatch update approximation trades update frequency for parallel execution. Titans adds momentum-like memory and forgetting; Nested Learning generalizes multiple update timescales and proposes Hope. These papers motivate **explicit adaptation protocols**, not a license to fit repeatedly on held-out manuscript answers. [TTT, A13](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.04620v1), [Titans, A14](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.00663v1), [Nested Learning, A15](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.24695v1).

If tested, freeze outer parameters and the adaptation rule, reset fast state at declared boundaries, allow only the observed prefix to update memory, and score unseen suffixes once. Save fast weights, momentum and gates for replay. An intervention must restore the **entire adaptive state**, otherwise apparent causal effects may simply arise from inconsistent optimizer memory. More powerful adaptation can memorize local frequencies without identifying a reusable cipher. We should start with TTT-Linear or a simple online probabilistic estimator before a deep self-modifying memory stack. A15 remains abstract-level here.

## Token-free does not mean assumption-free

BLT's entropy-guided patches and H-Net's jointly learned chunk boundaries demonstrate alternatives to fixed subword vocabularies. H-Net also provides causal routing/smoothing machinery. Their language-model experiments use hundreds of millions to billions of parameters and vast external corpora. Learned chunks can optimize prediction without corresponding to historical glyphs, morphemes or words. [BLT, A16](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.09871v1), [H-Net, A17](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.07955v1).

Our reversible transcription units are already closer to character modeling than an English BPE tokenizer. The relevant new question is whether uncertain multi-character glyph groupings should be modeled as latent segmentation. Proposed ablations should preserve original positions, provide fixed/random/space-based segmentation controls, and score the same original symbols. A chunker must be causal: changing an unseen suffix cannot change the represented prefix. Do not replace EVA with UTF-8 bytes and claim to have removed transcription assumptions. A16's rendered page contains a 2026 footer despite its 2024 version header; it is not counted as an independently established 2026 update.

## Objectives and scarce data deserve as much work as architecture

MTP can encourage shared features useful for several future positions, but independent horizon predictions do not specify their **joint** distribution. Our failed RRXOR/copy diagnostics make that distinction operational. A recurrent predictor can compute a joint continuation probability by updating state on each hypothetical continuation; the evaluation must cover combinations that have identical individual marginals. DeepSeek's teacher-forced sequential MTP modules, the original multi-head MTP paper, and our simpler auxiliary head are different implementations. [A18](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.19737v1).

Looped transformers share parameters across depth; extra loops add computation rather than examples. They are useful only if fresh-task recovery or length generalization improves under both parameter-matched and compute-matched comparisons. Hook each iteration separately, and distinguish repeated processing of a fixed prefix from recurrent updates as new observations arrive. [A19](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.17416v1).

The 2023 data-constrained scaling work supports diminishing returns from repeated data under its tested recipes, not a universal maximum number of epochs. More recent evidence makes **regularization a serious research axis**. A June 2026 preprint studies an auxiliary masked-input next-token loss on top of tuned strong weight decay, using 72M–1.4B models and 100M unique tokens. It reports improvements across matched seeds, but uses extra training computation and evaluates at a much larger data scale than Voynich. Its AR/diffusion comparison also uses different likelihood estimators and checkpoint conventions. [A20](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.16264v1), [MIR, A23](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.06888v1).

Project proposal: a small, bounded regularization grid with clean AR, tuned weight decay, input masking, and their combination. Match target exposure and separately compare elapsed compute. Preserve the clean-input objective and score uncorrupted held-out text. Masking ambiguous cipher observations could erase precisely the transition evidence we need, so include synthetic mechanism recovery as a co-primary outcome. Do not transplant a published weight-decay optimum or a fitted “extra-data equivalent” to the manuscript. Sparse repeated-training work is also worth tracking, but A26 is abstract-only and not yet a reason to add MoE.

## Causal inspectability and the 64 GB Mac

| Candidate | What can be inspected | Principal cost or confound | Adoption gate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Explicit probabilistic transitions | Belief vector, transition/emission tables, normalization | Chosen state family can be wrong or unidentifiable | Recover known ambiguous controls and transfer to fresh keys without truth-label fitting |
| Small nonlinear RNN | State before/after every symbol; recurrent Jacobian | Distributed features and saturation | Beat simple probabilistic and transformer controls on joint futures |
| Signed DeltaNet / DeltaProduct | Keys, writes, gates, transition factors | Extra factors are extra freedom; no native MPS kernel guarantee | Sequential CPU reference and MPS parity before speed claims |
| GDN/KDA plus short attention | Recurrent state and token-addressed branch | Credit can migrate between branches | Branch ablations and matched full-attention reference |
| Local lookup branch | Exact addresses, collisions and residual contribution | Memorization of transcription n-grams | Held-out key transfer and frequency-matched controls |
| TTT memory | Fast weights and update dynamics | Inner-loop state complicates restoration and leakage | Prefix-only adaptation with complete state reset/replay |
| Learned chunking | Boundary decisions and original-symbol alignment | Boundaries can encode predictive artifacts | Causal prefix invariance and fixed/random chunk controls |

These are proposed engineering gates. A 64 GB memory ceiling is not a throughput guarantee. The current campaign's low memory use means we can compare many independent seeds and mechanisms without forcing huge models into RAM. Plain small recurrent matrices are feasible candidates; optimized CUDA/Triton paper kernels require separate implementation or a measured fallback on MPS. Float32 training/analysis and a CPU reference remain preferable until profiling justifies precision changes. Frontier cache reductions at million-token contexts must not be turned into laptop speed estimates.

## Bounded next decision

The high-value architectural study is a **factorial diagnostic**, not a single model containing every new component: update family × objective (next-symbol/joint-future) × observation ambiguity, with a separate small regularization ablation. Start from fresh synthetic mechanisms; retain manuscript final holdouts. Promote a component only when it improves transferable rule recovery and survives causal controls. Larger context, fancier memory, or lower prediction loss alone is insufficient.

Coverage limitations: no frontier result was independently replicated; no paper here validates ciphertext-only Voynich decoding; not every proof or appendix was read. The source ledger is the authoritative reading-depth record. Source review remains distinct from accepted experiment registration.
