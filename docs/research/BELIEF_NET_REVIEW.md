**What the paper establishes (§§2–4; appendices B/C).** It learns a time-homogeneous categorical HMM: row-softmax logits represent initial, transition and emission probabilities; exact recurrent Bayesian filtering is differentiated through next-observation cross-entropy with AdamW. Emissions depend on current hidden state. State count is supplied for the main benchmark, then swept over 4–256 in a separate misspecification study. “Under/overcomplete” means states fewer/more than observable categories, not under/overfitting. Synthetic data use 64 states, 128 or 32 categories, 256-length sequences, strongly cyclic transitions and sharp random emissions. Validation uses the same generator parameters. Reported advantages are versus their Baum-Welch implementation; transformers often predict better. Parameter checks use transition eigenvalue magnitudes and emission-row optimal transport. Federalist Papers experiments assess character prediction and emission patterns. Baum-Welch is capped at 20 iterations; neural methods use minibatches and validation-selected settings. Appendix B.4 explicitly leaves recovery-specific convergence/sample-complexity guarantees open. [Full paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.10571v2).

**Our assessment.** Yes: a worthwhile rule-explicit synthetic comparator, because every prediction has an inspectable probabilistic update. It supplies the HMM family as an inductive assumption; success would not discover that family automatically or identify a historical writing process. Matching an eigenvalue spectrum or emissions separately is weaker than recovering one jointly aligned transition/emission mechanism. State permutations, duplicate states and observationally equivalent models require equivalence-aware evaluation; validation plateaus cannot uniquely identify state count. The presented timings should not be treated as an algorithm-independent speed guarantee.

For a future registered comparison, retain fresh key/family holdouts, hidden labels only for scoring, fixed state-count selection on development data, multiple initializations, equal exposure/compute accounting, tuned EM controls, IID/nonidentifiable cases, and multi-step predictive tests. EXP-0008's null/state generators couple emission and transition: a categorical HMM with the same state count may be misspecified; use an explicit edge-emitting model or a verified state augmentation. Copy-lag and other alternatives need separately justified models. Audit the objective carefully: equation (2) omits the first-observation term, while §3.3 compares with full joint likelihood. No current manuscript result changes because of this review.
