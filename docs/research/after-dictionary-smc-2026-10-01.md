For proposals, the next useful comparison is observation-informed conditional updates. Under a uniform conditional prior, a free-row heat-bath update has code probability G_t(K_with_row=u)/sum_v G_t(K_with_row=v). The old positive key makes the denominator positive. Exact enumeration of42codes gives a target-invariant Gibbs kernel, avoiding rejected zero-target codes and assigning weight to observed compatibility. One-row heat-bath can still be trapped by coupled support constraints; block updates are a separate problem, with42²candidates for two rows.

A cheaper ciphertext-informed code proposal requires the reverse/forward proposal ratio. Proposals must retain all supported codes, preserve known rows, and use ciphertext/current key/source information only. If a proposal depends on the current key, its full reverse probability must be evaluable; arbitrary repairing or best-code selection is not an invariant posterior step.
