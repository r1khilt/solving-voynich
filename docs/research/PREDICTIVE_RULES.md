# Candidate methods for explicit predictive rules

Post-registration source review, 2026-09-21. This was read while CAMPAIGN-0001 was running. It does not change that campaign's models, selection rules or thresholds. No method below has been implemented in this continuation.

**Source report — Ayache, Eyraud and Goudian (2019), _Explaining Black Boxes on Sequential Data using Weighted Automata_, PMLR93, pp.81–103.** Read sections2–3 and AppendixC in the [primary paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v93/ayache19a/ayache19a.pdf). Their algorithm queries a sequence-scoring model, constructs prefix/suffix Hankel matrices and extracts weighted transition matrices by truncated singular-value factorization. It approximates a predictor's sequence behavior; it is not a plaintext decoder. The appendix discusses negative predicted weights and the use of a small replacement value for scoring. Such validity failures must be measured, not hidden by favorable averages. Its recurrent-model experiments do not establish recovery of a historical generator from our transformer.

**Source report — Bailly, Carreras and Quattoni (2013), _Unsupervised Spectral Learning of Finite State Transducers_, NeurIPS26.** Read the introduction, sample construction and experiment description in the [primary paper](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2013/file/e836d813fd184325132fca8edcdfb40e-Paper.pdf). The method learns from paired input/output strings whose internal alignment is unknown. The word “unsupervised” does not mean the output strings are absent. Voynich provides no equivalent paired plaintext, so this paper cannot be presented as a ready ciphertext-only solution.

**Inference / candidate follow-up.** A useful successor to blind clustering would infer explicit symbol-conditioned state updates and then predict held-out *joint future strings*. That would demand more than agreement with three separate future-symbol marginals or a clustering score. Compare direct finite-state fitting with model-derived rules on fresh synthetic generators, record nonidentifiable alternatives, and reserve unseen keys/families. Any future run needs its own source review, finite budget, negative controls and registration. Similarity to the manuscript would still identify a predictive description rather than a unique historical encoding.
