# Can a 100M-parameter model learn from one manuscript?

**Research memo, 2026-09-23 UTC.** This is a design assessment, not a run, a preregistration, a TEACH-0003 result, or a Voynich reading. No interim TEACH-0003 outputs were inspected. The comparison is between (a) learning a large predictor *from this manuscript alone* and (b) learning an inference procedure from many independently generated or known communication systems, then conditioning it on the manuscript. The second is scientifically more promising, but only if transfer survives genuinely new systems and the manuscript has evidence that separates their explanations.

## The actual amount of evidence

The repository's immutable [ZL3b preparation manifest](../../../data/manifests/zl3b_preparation.json) records 227 source pages, one empty excluded page, and a leaf-separated split of 177 training pages (81 leaves; 184,930 tokens including BOS/EOS), 24 validation pages (10 leaves; 26,371 tokens), and 25 untouched test pages (10 leaves; 22,342 tokens). The train text has 184,576 codepoints, and the reversible tokenizer has 112 units. These are **transcription units**, not independently sampled words or semantic labels; repeated passages, page/section structure, uncertainty, and line conventions reduce effective independent evidence. The test split remains unscored and cannot become a convenient development set. The existing [architecture review](../ARCHITECTURE_REVIEW.md) and [world-model/diffusion synthesis](../world-models-diffusion-2026-09-21/SYNTHESIS.md) already argue for this distinction.

At 100 million parameters, the training split supplies only about **0.00185 unique training tokens per parameter**, or about 541 parameters per observed training token. This ratio is descriptive, not a theorem: a small glyph vocabulary and shared linguistic regularities differ from web text. [Hoffmann et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.15556) fit compute-optimal trends on large natural-language corpora, and [Muennighoff et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.16264) show that repeated data eventually has diminishing value in their much larger data-constrained regime. Neither law can be numerically transplanted to a 112-unit manuscript. But repeating these 185,000 units millions of times does not create independent observations about what their referents are.

A large manuscript-only model **can** learn something: glyph frequencies, local dependencies, section and scribe differences, copying, transcription conventions, and perhaps longer-range predictive structure. It could be useful as a *diagnostic* or a strong likelihood component if held-out leaf predictions beat matched n-gram, compression, copy/mutate, and smaller-neural baselines. It cannot acquire a grounded plaintext lexicon merely by growing in parameter count. Earlier local evidence is modest: [EXP-0002](../../experiments/EXP-0002-results.md) found a training/validation gap and no reliable advantage from its larger models under that schedule; [EXP-0010](../../experiments/EXP-0010-results.md) found no registered win from longer context, with only four pages providing full 2,048-unit histories. These results do **not** test 100M parameters or exhaust optimization. They do show why an increase in capacity must be tested against independent observations, rather than treated as additional evidence.

## Where a transferable prior could come from

The more defensible 100M+ objective is **meta-inference**: across many systems, learn how to infer an unfamiliar system from a short support document, and test on independent systems. [MAML](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v70/finn17a.html) establishes an optimization route for quick adaptation across tasks; [Garg et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.01066) show in-context learning on specified synthetic function classes. Neither result shows transfer from arbitrary simulated scripts to historical writing. [DreamCoder](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.08381) illustrates a complementary idea: reusable, executable program abstractions learned across tasks. The earlier [world-model synthesis](../world-models-diffusion-2026-09-21/SYNTHESIS.md) proposed document-producing worlds; the narrower recommendation here is to require **compilable hypotheses and out-of-family recovery before any Voynich deployment**.

An episode would sample a document producer $H=(L,C,P,V,S)$: a linguistic or nonlinguistic message process $L$, a writing/cipher/abbreviation channel $C$, a page/layout process $P$, an optional image-to-content relation $V$, and section/scribe variation $S$. It would emit a support document $D_{1:m}$ and held-out leaves $D_{m+1:m+k}$, along with the known generating program and, where meaningful, the plaintext/relations. A neural encoder $q_\phi(H\mid D_{1:m})$ proposes **multiple** typed candidate programs with uncertainty. A separately implemented executor evaluates $p_H(D_{m+1:m+k}\mid D_{1:m})$ and exact constraints; a bounded search or particle update revises the candidates. If latent message, key, segmentation, and layout must change together, an iterative denoiser could propose joint revisions, but it earns its place only against beam/MCMC/program-search controls under the **same executor and compute budget**. The compiler, not fluent generated text, defines the decoder.

The proposed training signal is mixed: supervised program and latent-state recovery on constructed worlds; held-out-leaf prediction; calibration of posterior mass on observationally equivalent alternatives; and a reject/abstain action when no family fits. Training worlds must include language, ciphers, abbreviation, copy/mutate, finite-state notation, structured nonsemantic text, and random controls. They must vary *mechanisms*, not only names, keys, token IDs, or rendering style. Use at least one independently authored generator suite plus real known-language/known-cipher documents that the training simulator did not produce. Even that coverage cannot make the prior historically complete.

At manuscript time, **freeze neural weights and program grammar first**. Infer a posterior over candidate $H$ values from the 177 training pages, optionally refining only low-dimensional document-specific variables such as a key or scribe mixture. No plaintext labels exist for this document. A second stage may condition on scans, layout and uncertain transcription, but those are correlated views of the same pages; they are not extra independent manuscripts. A blank-image or shuffled-image control must expose visual leakage. A pretrained language model may be an external *prior* component, but its English fluency is not manuscript evidence; model/version and contamination audit must be recorded.

This is a deliberately **hierarchical** architecture: a learned proposal network amortizes search, whereas explicit programs carry the historical claims. It need not be a single 100M-parameter end-to-end transformer. A frozen large pretrained encoder plus a smaller trainable proposal/executor interface may be preferable if it beats the same controls. A diffusion process is optional inference machinery, not an independent source of information.

## Identifiability: what the model still cannot know

Suppose two hypotheses $H_1,H_2$ induce the same distribution over all observed text, layout and image evidence: $p(D,I\mid H_1)=p(D,I\mid H_2)$. Their likelihood ratio is one, so no corpus-only inference algorithm can distinguish them; the posterior odds equal the prior odds. On a finite document the practical problem is harsher: different generators can be *approximately* indistinguishable. A substitution and an invented lexicon may also differ only by a renaming symmetry. [Locatello et al.](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v97/locatello19a.html) prove a related limitation for unsupervised factor recovery; the equation here is this project's direct likelihood argument, not their theorem applied to Voynich.

Thus the system must report **equivalence classes**, posterior sensitivity to the prior, and exactly which external anchor breaks a symmetry. A high-probability candidate is not a reading if a nonsemantic producer or another language/channel combination predicts the same held-out marks. Image alignment only identifies semantics if the visual-text relation was specified prospectively and beats image-permutation, topic-only and independent-channel controls. If every proposed program permits arbitrary null deletion or page-specific exceptions, its likelihood is meaningless without a complexity penalty and a fixed exception budget.

Prior decipherment work illustrates the assumption load. [Knight et al. 2006](https://aclanthology.org/P06-2065/) explicitly model plaintext and cipher channels on several known tasks; [Ravi and Knight 2011](https://aclanthology.org/P11-1002/) treat foreign text as a cipher for English using a language model. [Hauer and Kondrak 2016](https://aclanthology.org/Q16-1006/) apply an anagrammed-substitution assumption to Voynich and report a language suggestion; that conditional result is not independent evidence that the assumed process generated the manuscript. [Reddy and Knight 2011](https://aclanthology.org/W11-1511/) analyze Voynich structure without a translation. A learned prior can accelerate search **inside** a family; it cannot validate that family's historical premise by producing plausible output.

## A decisive evaluation ladder

1. **Generator-disjoint calibration.** Freeze development and final families at the *mechanism* level: leave out whole grammar/channel compositions, new independently coded producer implementations, and new writing/visual conventions. No shared template strings, key tables, underlying plaintexts, or pseudo-random seed lineage across splits. Compare 10-, 50-, and 177-page support budgets at approximately the manuscript's unit count. Evaluate exact program recovery **modulo observational equivalence**, decoded relations/plaintext where ground truth exists, withheld-leaf log score and code length, top-$k$ coverage, calibrated abstention, and family-cluster intervals. A system that only handles fresh keys from familiar producers has not passed this gate.
2. **Controls and ablations.** Compare the same inference budget with a no-meta-training model, a manuscript-only model, a pretrained generic LM prior, n-grams/compressors, copy/mutate and explicit cipher/program search. Add shuffled world labels, a restricted English-template simulator, independent null labels, permuted image-page associations, layout-only inputs, glyph relabeling, and a no-language/nonsemantic world. The neural proposer must improve exact recovery or posterior efficiency **at a matched executor and search budget**, not merely produce prettier explanations. Run two or more seeds; preserve failures and all candidates, not just the winner.
3. **Real-world transfer before Voynich.** Test on complete known-language manuscripts or independently documented historical ciphers with a withheld answer key, using the same input size and uncertain transcription format. Freeze architecture and hyperparameters before this test. A known family in another font is too easy; include channels or linguistic structures outside the simulator's training families. Report where the method abstains and whether its confidence detects misspecification.
4. **Voynich prospective test.** Condition on the 177 training pages; the much-used 24-page validation set can guide a *registered* selection rule but cannot serve as fresh confirmation. Score the existing 25-page, 10-leaf untouched test **once** after freezing the entire model, prior, search budget, transcription choices and candidate-selection rule. Report all leaves, section/scribe strata and uncertainty, compare exact code length/log score to strong surface models, and disclose any overlap or contamination. A likelihood win alone is a predictive result. A semantic claim additionally needs a fixed, independently applied decoder with cross-page consistency and an external historical or visual anchor unavailable during selection, as required by the [protocol](../PROTOCOL.md).

A candidate **go/no-go rule to register later**, before generating any evaluation worlds, is that the meta-model improve out-of-family exact program recovery by at least 10 percentage points over the best compute-matched non-meta control in each of two seeds; maintain at least 90% coverage of the true *equivalence class* in its nominal 90% credible sets; and reject at least 90% of prespecified out-of-support nonsemantic cases while retaining at least 80% of identifiable positives. These numbers are proposals, not tuned thresholds or claims of feasibility. If the generator-disjoint and real-known-system stages fail, do not spend the final Voynich holdout to rescue the narrative. If they pass but Voynich alternatives remain observationally tied, report that limit rather than a translation.

## Local resource envelope and decision

For a **100M trainable-parameter float32 AdamW** model, weights, gradients and two optimizer moments alone need about $100\mathrm{M}\times16$ bytes = **1.6 GB decimal**; activations, attention, data, search particles and allocator overhead can dominate. The user's 64 GB unified-memory M5 Pro is plausibly sufficient for a modest context and batch, but not established to support a full document-posterior model at useful speed. As one *planning envelope*, 10,000 updates at batch 16 and 512 units expose about **81.9M generated units** and have a dense-transformer training proxy of roughly $6ND\approx4.9\times10^{16}$ floating-point operations before attention and executor costs. A six-hour cap would require an average below **2.16 seconds/update including overhead**. The existing TEACH-0003 benchmark used 14-token synthetic inputs, so its step time cannot validate this 512-unit proposal. Require a source-matched 32–100-step MPS benchmark, peak-allocation measurement and a conservative full-run time projection before registration; cap local allocation (for example 24 GiB sampled MPS), elapsed time (six hours), steps, search expansions and checkpoint size. Do not launch an unbounded 100M run on this memo's estimate.

The highest-value first investment is not another manuscript-only parameter jump. It is **independent generator construction and a frozen transfer benchmark**; then a small/medium/100M capacity comparison on the *same* task distribution and search budget can say whether scale helps. If 100M improves surface loss but fails mechanism-disjoint recovery, that is a useful result about predictive modeling, not decipherment. If it recovers new known systems and calibrated uncertainty, it becomes a credible inference instrument, though its Voynich conclusion can still remain “unidentified.”
